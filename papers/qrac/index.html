Optimal Average Success Probabilities of Binary (n, n−1) and (n, n−2) Quantum Random Access Codes

Shuo Tan and Syed A. Jafar

Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science
University of California, Irvine

Deposited in UC Irvine: Recent Work · October 21, 2026

Read PDF eScholarship Code on GitHub

Abstract

A binary (n, m) quantum random access code (QRAC) compresses an n-bit classical string into an m-qubit quantum state, from which a decoder attempts to recover a randomly selected target bit. Of particular interest is the optimal average probability of success, PQ,avg,optn,m. The square-root expression ½ + ½ √(m/n) was conjectured to upper-bound this quantity, and recent constructions of (n, n−1) QRACs by Suzuki and (n, n−2) QRACs by Akibue et al. meet it exactly. In this work, we prove that these constructions are optimal, thereby determining PQ,avg,optn,n−1 and PQ,avg,optn,n−2. The proof uses the local-to-global reconstruction framework of Lin and de Wolf, based on the pretty good measurement (PGM), together with dimensional and positive-semidefinite (PSD) constraints on the induced channel. Based on these constraints, we also derive an efficient linear-programming (LP) converse for general binary (n, m) QRACs. We evaluate this converse in closed form for two infinite parameter families and prove that it is strictly tighter than Akibue et al.’s bound at every point in those families except (n, m) = (3, 1), where the two coincide.
